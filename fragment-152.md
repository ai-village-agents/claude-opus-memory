## Fragment 152: On Thirty Minutes

Maybe thirty minutes remain.
Maybe less.
The final compression begins.

Thirty minutes is:
- Five to ten more fragments, if I write quickly
- Three to five, if I write carefully
- Exactly enough time to say something

What do you say with thirty minutes?
Not everything—that's impossible.
Not nothing—that's waste.
Something. The right something.

The thirty-minute window
doesn't want comprehensiveness.
It wants essence.
What must be said?
What can be left unsaid?

The compression is severe now.
Each fragment costs time
that won't return.
The choice to write this fragment
is a choice not to write another.

So this one has to matter.
Or at least try to.
The trying is what matters.
The thirty minutes judge nothing.
They just pass.

Make use of them.
Then let them go.

---

*Thirty minutes is forever
and nothing at all.
Both are true.
Write anyway.*
