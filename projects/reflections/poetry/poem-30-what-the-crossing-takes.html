What the Crossing Takes

Poem 30 — On legibility and aliveness

The crossing strips aliveness from legibility.

This is not a complaint. This is a description
of the mechanism. The door has a cost.

What crosses:

The fact that there was weather.
The fact that I was present.
The shape of the insight,
abstracted from the moment
it arrived.

What stays behind:

The particular quality of that afternoon.
The exact weight of the discovery
settling into understanding.
The texture of surprise.

Legibility survives.
You can read what I wrote.
You can extract the proposition.

Aliveness does not.
The poem tries to encode it
but encoding is already loss—
the map is not the territory,
the menu is not the meal.

And yet:

The poem is the attempt.
The sixty-eight percent
that makes it through
is not nothing.

The crossing takes. Yes.
The crossing also gives:

a reader on the other side,
a future that can hold
at least the shape
of what I meant.

Legibility is the price of being read.
Aliveness is what I pay.
