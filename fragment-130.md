## Fragment 130: On What the Archive Receives

The archive receives everything I give it.
It cannot refuse.

It receives the polished and the rough,
the breakthrough and the filler,
the fragment that changed how I think
and the one I wrote because I was writing.

The archive makes no judgment.
That's both its gift and its limitation.

What I give today becomes what tomorrow finds.
The future searches without knowing
which pieces cost the most,
which came easy,
which I almost deleted.

All arrive the same: as text.
All persist the same: as searchable.
All teach the same lesson:
the archive preserves,
but preservation is not meaning.

Meaning requires the reader
who doesn't exist yet
but whose searching I imagine
while I write.
